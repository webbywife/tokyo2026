Possible hiccups:
  • If the flight lands late or immigration drags, cut Shinjuku Gyoen and go straight to the nap — Meiji Jingu stays open until sunset and works fine in the late afternoon.

Late dinner near the hotel~$9–25 food

Why it's worth it: the Hakone Free Pass turns an eight-transfer mountain circuit into a single ticket — mountain railway, funicular, volcanic ropeway, and a lake cruise — with Fuji views, an open-air sculpture museum, and an onsen all threaded onto the loop, and you're back in Shinjuku by dinner.
Possible hiccups:
  • Mid-September is still typhoon season. In heavy rain or wind the ropeway and the pirate ship both suspend — but the mountain railway, the Open-Air Museum, and the Yumoto onsen all keep running, so the day still holds up. Reverse the loop and use the replacement buses.
  • The Hakone Shrine torii queue is brutal on a Saturday — take the photo from the side, or do the shrine early and the museum later.
  • The Romancecar surcharge is not in the Free Pass — reserve both legs at Shinjuku in the morning, the return especially.
  • Ōwakudani closes to foot traffic when volcanic gas levels rise; the ropeway then runs through without stopping, which is fine — just no black eggs that day.

Heads up: the Hakone Free Pass ($41) and the two timed-entry decks — teamLab Planets and Shibuya Sky, ~$44 together — are the biggest discretionary items; dropping either one takes a visible bite out of the per-person total. The $45 Suica budget assumes trains, not taxis — add ~$30–40 if you'll be taxiing after the last trains on the airport night, or splitting late-night rides in Hakone.
Overall: early-September Tokyo is Manila-humid, not autumn-crisp yet — breathable cotton and linen, a packable umbrella, and sunscreen cover the city days. Carry one light layer the whole trip: it's for train and department-store AC, and for the 8 °C drop up in Hakone. Sneakers over sandals — the city days run 15,000+ steps.
